We are all being pushed to work faster and with more automation than ever before. With this, we are increasingly experiencing failures in our processes and team cultures. One of the marks of a good system, be it technological, biological, or social, is how it reacts to those failures and what kinds of protections are in place to catch problems before they become critical. Not every failure point revolves around humans, but these are often the most complex and sensitive to plan for. We will cover common cognitive biases and how they lay the foundations for categories of mistakes we can all make. Then we’ll discuss structural improvements such as checklist culture and objective measurements which can fight against those biases. And finally we’ll go through how we can support each other with healthy review processes, as well as looking at the ways that unhealthy processes can lead to burnout.
